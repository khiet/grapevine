Worth keeping

Three directions

They are ordered by how much they change. A fixes the system and keeps the layout. B reorganises the list around what waits on you. C trades cards and avatars for density and the keyboard. The mocks show the same fixture PRs and scroll like the real list.

Mock theme

Design A

Tidy

Same layout, same sections, same marks. Everything is put on a token system, a three-step radius scale and a three-size type scale, and the contrast and target-size failures are fixed. You could ship this without anyone needing to relearn the app.

Best ifyou like the current design and want it consistent and accessible.
Trade-offyour-turn rows get louder but still live inside Mine and Participated.
Sizemostly App.css; about two days.

List. The first row is shown hovered.

Settings, with one button ranking.

Design B

Your turn

The popover opens on one pinned section holding exactly the PRs the tray number counts. Each row leads with a state tile and says in words why it is there, so nothing needs a tooltip. The accent becomes the icon's grape, and row actions get a labelled bar.

Best ifthe job of the popover is "what do I do next" more than "show me everything".
Trade-offfaces move to a small supporting role; a your-turn PR leaves its home section.
SizepopoverView.ts, PrList.tsx, CSS; about a week with tests.

The second row is shown hovered, with its action bar.

Design C

Compact

Tabs replace the four collapsible sections, rows drop to a fixed 46px with one-line titles, and the cards go away. Ten PRs fit where six or seven do today. Every action has a key, and the footer shows the keys.

Best ifyou watch many repos and open the popover with a global shortcut.
Trade-offlong titles truncate (full title on hover); the look is plainer and less Mac-like.
Sizenew tab and selection state plus key handling; about a week and a half.

The second row is the keyboard selection.

Suggested mix

If you want a default: take all of A as the foundation, then B1, B2, B4 and B5 for the attention model and the brand, then C5 and C7 for the keyboard and the footer. That leaves cards and two-line titles in place and skips C's tabs.

Picks that conflict

B1 and C1 both restructure the sections; choose one. B3 and C3 both demote the avatar in different ways. B2 and C4 are two spellings of the same idea (states in words); B2 keeps pills and tiles, C4 is dot plus text. Everything in A combines with anything.
